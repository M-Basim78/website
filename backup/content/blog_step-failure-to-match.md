### Overcoming USMLE Step One Failures to Residency Success

Saturday, March 15, 2025

Watch Video on Below Post

Welcome to our exploration of resilience and perseverance in the journey of a medical career. I am Dr. Moss, a psychiatry resident, and I want to share my personal story of overcoming a USMLE Step One failure. This story is not just about failure; it’s about tenacity, introspection, and finding strength in adversity.

My journey through medical school was fueled by passion, determination, and sacrifice, like many of you might experience. Amidst the pressures of the STEP One exam, compounded by the onset of COVID-19, I faced a significant hurdle. Despite preparation and determination, my first STEP One attempt ended in failure by just one point. Yet, this moment of despair became the foundation for my eventual success. Facing this setback, I learned an important lesson: pause, reflect, and regroup. It's easy to want to dive back into studying, but taking time to process emotions and reassess strategies is crucial. Managing emotional health, financial stress, and the pressure to succeed were pivotal challenges. I emphasize the importance of acknowledging these struggles and not letting them dictate your journey.

Dealing with Failure

It took time to process my emotions. I had to acknowledge my disappointment before I could move forward. After taking a short break, I devised a new study plan and retook Step 1 in a few months, restructuring my study schedule and going through STEP accommodations, which takes 60 days to receive a response. This journey was painful but necessary for growth.

The second time I failed was because I was on LOA and working 40+ hours a week. For my third attempt, I was JUST STUDYING, which allowed me to finally pass.

The journey to residency is not defined by past failures but by how you learn and grow from them. I advise applicants to maintain a holistic approach to applications and honestly address past failures as a learning experience. This approach builds a narrative of resilience and strength.

Overcoming Red Flags in Residency Applications

While I can’t provide specific advice without knowing your full application, failing exams can be approached like failing STEP. Explain what you learned from the experience and how you grew from it. There is a section on ERAS that asks about broad academic challenges, where you can provide this context.

**Q: I passed Step 1 recently on my first attempt but worry about how my transcript will be perceived. I failed several exams while in medical school, which is a “red flag.” What should I do?**

First, I acknowledged my failure in my application but reframed it as a learning experience. There’s no denying that failing Step 1 makes matching more challenging, but it doesn’t make it impossible. Certain competitive specialties (specifically surgical) may be more challenging to enter, but it depends on the entire application. A strong clinical performance, research, and networking can offset a Step failure. Coming from a T10 school can help because of strong institutional support and connections, but it’s still not a guarantee.

**Q: Based on the fact you are in residency with two failed Step 1 attempts, I’m wondering why those in medicine convince us a failed Step 1 attempt makes matching impossible? That being said, I helped multiple IMGs this match 2024 cycle with their apps. Those who applied only to Psych received 1-3 interviews. Those who applied to IM had more interviews (more than 3). My story exemplifies that setbacks do not define one's trajectory. With introspection, strategic planning, and leaning on support systems, you can overcome failures and find success in your medical career. Remember, you're not alone on this journey. Keep striving, and find strength in your resilience.
