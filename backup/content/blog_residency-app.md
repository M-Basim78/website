### Choosing a Specialty:

Questions to ask yourself:

Where am I the most happy?

Where does time go by quickly?

What type of patients do I gravitate to?

Where do I enjoy learning outside of studying?

What types of practicing doctors do I relate most to?

Which specialty do my personal values and passions align?

What do people I care about, observe my enthusiasm peak as I delve into the topic?

What do I love?

Don’t let other’s expectations or realities influence your pursuit of your dreams. So keep learning, keep fighting, and don’t limit your imagination in order to accomplish your dreams!

Is there a specific population, age range, or demographic?

“There is not one population I just love. I value people with disabilities through living with my brother-in law and while working at Imagine as a case manager. I value working with older patients and those close to death, who rarely have anyone to advocate for them. I also want to work with kids who have been abused to help show them they can make it”

“I want to help people who are disempowered, disenfranchised, and not usually given the same amount of resources and focus and care as others. This includes Hispanics and blacks, LGBTQ+, trauma patients, females, and homeless populations. I want to address their whole body, mind, and soul. I want to help people who have had trauma. This includes helping them get social work and support for food, living, healthcare and all the basic needs they deserve. “ 4/2021

What is my purpose in medicine?

"I can’t quite say it is one thing but it is an accumulation: To medically take care of others. To take care of people as people that includes their mental state, their life, and physical body. I know it is a bit ambitious but I know it can be done even if it is just for a couple people." 6/2021

"I have found my voice and I have the power to share my ideas, experiences, and lessons I have learned with others.” 5/2021

“That is what I can advocate for, no matter who the person is all they want in life is to be seen and heard.“ 12/2019

“The practice of viewing health from a psychological and trauma informed lens is called integrative medicine. This is why I am pursuing my medical degree, so I can help others look back at the various threats they have and are currently encountering and see how they are creating negative impacts on their health. Through looking for the 'root causes' one can pursue better informed health care decisions and management.”

Constant re-evaluation of Personal Values, Goals, and Passions

Speak with Physician Mentors, Residents, Family, and Partner

M4 Electives

- Choose electives passionate about

- Choose ones from each specialty if applying to multiple specialties

Step2

Take Exam and Get Score before Applications are due in September

- Many people take it in May - July

- Takes around 4 weeks to get score

- People study between 2 - 8weeks

I studied in 6 weeks in June and July after a 2 week vacation and a 2 week Elective

- DO NOT APPLY WITHOUT SCORE

I know individuals who applied without score and failed which lead them to need to take an LOA and re-apply the following year

Lessons learned from failing STEP1

- Read Blog Post:

[Lessons Learned from Failing USMLE STEP1: Comprehensive Guide](https://medpsycmoss.com/blog/how-to-not-fail-step)

I Scored: 229

Sub-i and Away Rotations

- Choose Sub-internship in specialty want to go into

- Usual options are Surgery, IM, FM, Peds

- If don't have official Sub-i then do second Sub-i in specialty

For example: I did one in In-patient Psychiatry, my husband did one in Neuro-ICU

- Like a long interview

- Work with residents and attending in a team

- Practice doing Consults, Putting in Orders, and Calling families

- Usually have to pay for travel and lodging

- Sometimes can increase student loans for this

- Interviews are NOT A GUARANTEE

Mine:

County Hospital in Family Medicine In-Patient floor

Home Institution in Adult In-patient Psychiatry

I did not do any away rotations in Psychiatry but wanted to if I had more time and $

Conferences

- Go to ones in both specialties

- Go to Residency fairs

- focus on programs planning on applying to

- Send thank you emails

- Regional Ones: All the Midwest Programs and especially ones in Chicago

- Volunteer or apply for funding for free or discounted entrance and lodging

- Present research posters, talks, and submit abstracts

### Personal Statement

Important things to know:

- Most reviewers just get PS

- Use PS to say YES/NO on an interview

- Most people who interview you will ONLY get the PS which they will use to talk about YOU

- Should NOT be a rewording of your CV or activities

Personal Story and Journey

- WHO are YOU?

- What could you do a TED Talk About?

- What Life experiences have impacted who you are as a person, future doctor, and collegue?

- What keeps you motivated in medicine?

- What are your values?

- What were my key areas of growth during medical school?

- Why YOU?

- through an activity, patient interaction, or experience

### Dual Applying to Residency:

Whether or not to Dual Apply

Pros:

- Increase chance of getting an interview

- Increased number of programs ranked

- Increased chance of matching

[NRMP data shows average number of matched MD applicants ranked 12 programs](https://www.nrmp.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Impact-Data_2024.pdf)

- Increase Chance of Ending up in Certain Location

Cons:

- More time and work to write both PS

- Interviewers might look down upon dual applying

- Might have to be more secretive on what program and specialty applying

- Especially publicly on social media or any published writings

Additional Info:

- One PS for Each Specialty

- ERAS app will be the SAME for both

- Have mentors from that specialty review it

### ERAS:

I created this to help you draft and plan your personal statement, 2024 ERAS application, and preparing for difficult interview questions on failures, challenges, and LOAs!

[Residency App Workbook](https://medpsycmoss.com/store/p_3291868/residency-application-workbook)

10 Activities:

- Don't write like a CV

- State key roles and responsibilities

- Can group similar activities into categories of advocacy, research or volunteering

- State a 1 liner describing the organization or activity

- Write key lessons or take aways from activity

Most meaningful:

- Talk about personal or professional growth

- How you will bring this experience to residency or as your future career

- Don't just restate what was in your PS, try to choose a different activity to speak on

Letter of Recommendations (LOR)

- Have letter writers from each specialty (if dual applying)

- 2 Psychiatrists write for Psychiatry Residency

- 2 Family Medicine Doctors write for Family Medicine Residency

- Have a couple mentors (IM) not in either Psychiatry or Family Medicine write two letters - one for each residency

- Provide letter writers CV and Personal Statement

- Submit 3-4 LOR per program (check if they are specific)

Psychiatry: 2 Psyc + 1 IM

Family Med: 1 FM + 1 FM Chair + 1 IM

Academic or Personal Challenges (New for 2025)

- This is the appropriate place to talk about any failures or leaves of absences

- Focus more on what you learned and took away from the experience

- Since you write about the challenges here - DO NOT talk about them in your PS

- Only the PD or APD usually receive this section

- They will have your STEP attempts, Transcripts, and MSPE so don't just state what it says there

Impactful Experiences box:

- Read blog post

["Exploring the Impactful Experiences Box"](https://medpsycmoss.com/blog/addressing-the-red-flags-in-my-residency-application)

- Make sure you talk about something you have not mentioned anywhere else in your application because space is limited

- Usually only the PD or APD read it

- Talk about challenges like finances, first generation, language barriers, race, SES, etc.
