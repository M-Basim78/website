What is a Disability?

Per the on the Disability Flag: "A is broadly defined as any condition of the body or mind (impairment) that makes it more difficult for the person with the condition to do certain activities (activity limitation) and interact with the world around them (participation restrictions)."

[Disabled World Article](https://www.disabled-world.com/disability/awareness/disability-flags.php#google_vignette)

disability

"There are many types of disabilities, such as those that affect a person's:

Vision

Hearing Thinking Learning Movement Mental health Remembering Communicating Social relationships"

Per "Demystifying Disabilities" by Emily Landau

- Having a disability is like not having a passcode to a room

- Providing accommodations to individuals with disabilities:

- It is “not about special treatment or privileges”

- “Remove barriers to participation and engagement so all people can fullest extent possible”

- “Making things more equitable” so all people can have the same opportunity to thrive

- Cause trauma when denied and seen as a burden

- It is the law to provide accessibility

- One does not need to explain or answer to anyone about their disability

My journey as a medical student with Disabilities:

At first, I was nervous to tell my residents and attending physicians that I had health accommodations. However, every time I told my team they would say it was no big deal and to just remind them that morning. For example, one resident told me: “Oh ya no worries! Get up and take a break whenever you need!” As long as I tell them in advance about my medical appointments and COMMUNICATE that day, then all is well! Per recommendation from the clinical director I scheduled my weekly health appointments consistently late in the afternoons since we usually round in the mornings. However, in some teams we are frequently dismissed before my set appointments so it works out that I don't even miss clinic or time on rotation.

If you have a medical or mental health condition while in medical school- get an accommodations letter from the disability’s office! It is not as scary as I once thought. This letter has made me feel more accepted and less self conscious to take the time to address my physical and medical health needs.

### What are the Laws Regarding Disabilities?

Disability rights are both civil and human rights.

The is a law that protects people with disabilities from discrimination across multiple aspects of their daily lives.

Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA)

makes it unlawful for an employer or educational establishment to discriminate against a qualified applicant, student, or employee with a disability. The ADA applies to private employers with 15 or more employees and to state and local government employers.

Title I of the Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990 (ADA)

The ADA defines an individual with a disability as a person who:

(1) has a physical or mental impairment that substantially limits a major life activity,

(2) has a record or history of a substantially limiting impairment, or

(3) is regarded or perceived by an employer as having a substantially limiting impairment.

The U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) enforces the employment provisions of the ADA and has a list of all the laws it enforces .

[here](https://www.eeoc.gov/statutes/laws-enforced-eeoc)

### How to Request Accommodations in Medical School

1. Contact your disability advocate or department

2. Write a letter about your journey including your struggles and challenges in previous educational environments.

3. Prepare any evidence of your challenges - grades, report cards, letters from teachers/doctors/therapists

### What are truly Essential Job Functions?

- Check out this

[article by Accessibility.com](https://www.accessibility.com/blog/what-are-essential-job-functions-under-the-ada)

- Per this article: "Essential functions are job duties that employees must have the ability to perform, with or without a reasonable accommodation."

- Disability Advocate Dr. Jarrell Stated in regards to this: "Often times, schools or programs will have technical standards that say you need to be able to stand for a certain period of time or lift something of a certain weight. In my mind, there is a big disconnect between those requirements and meeting an essential function of a job with or without accommodation. If a wheelchair is used, then the standing requirement may not be valid. This article says that employers need to list what they deem to be the essential functions and I believe this would apply to residency programs as well."

created a with the following information:

The US Equal Employment Opportunity Commissioner (EEOC)

[Facts Sheet](https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/job-applicants-and-ada#potential)

"The ADA prohibits employers from asking questions that are likely to reveal the existence of a disability before making a job offer (i.e., the pre-offer period). This prohibition covers written questionnaires and inquiries made during interviews, as well as medical examinations. However, such questions and medical examinations are permitted after extending a job offer but before the individual begins work (i.e., the post-offer period)."

### What are "Reasonable Accommodations?"

- Per the EEOC: "A reasonable accommodation can help a person with a disability apply for a job, perform the duties of a job, or enjoy the benefits and privileges of employment."

- Do they HAVE to provide?

- Yes!

- But the company/organization can be picky if it is too much $$ or causes too much "hardship" for the company....

- Here is a by the US Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC)

[resource page](https://www.eeoc.gov/eeoc-disability-related-resources/reasonable-accommodation)

### Examples of Reasonable Accommodations:

Mobility devices and assistants:

- Wheelchair

- Walker

- Cane

- Crutches

- Stool - helpful during clinical walking rounds & surgery rotation

- Rails

Help for limited visibility, balance needs, and support to not fall

Headphones

- To increase sound

- To decrease sound

- Noise cancelling

Communication Aids:

- Hearing aid/cochlear implant with Bluetooth/wireless

- Sign language interpreter

- A tablet or phone to type

- A white board

- A communication tablet

- Pictures of items or emotions

- Note taking

- Need Slide Deck before hand to review

Reading:

- Brail

- Large text

- Magnifying Glass

Quiet Rooms

- Assist with sensory overload

- Can have padding on walls to prevent harm to individual if they are moving around and hitting the wall

- Can contain:

- Blanket

- Cuddle pillow

- Sensory tools

- Coloring book

- Music

- White noise machine

- Candles or aromatherapy

- Space for sitting or laying on the floor

Animals

- Emotional support animals

- Need a letter from a trained Psychiatrist in your state

- Rental Properties can not charge a "pet fee" for ES animals

- Service Animals:

- Notify of an illness flare (seizure, low glucose)

- Visual assistance

- Don’t pet because distracts them

- Provide emergency Support

Travel

- Wheel chair vans

- Modified vehicles and bikes

- Parking Spots closer to the entrance

Bathrooms

- Not “handicap” bathroom

- Private space

- Closed room

- Privacy for medical device use

Medical Device Attached to a Person’s Body

- Catheter

- Colostomy Bag

- Continuous glucose monitor Including testing strips and packaging

- Heart rate monitor

- Insulin Pump

- Oxygen tank

- Spinal Cord Stimulator

- TENS Units

- Urine Drainage Bag Including cleaning supplies

Medicine and Medical Device

- Medicine as pills, tablets, fluid, injections, topical, suppositories, etc.

- Water

- Bandages

- Medical Arm/ Shoulder Sling

- Braces (Neck, Back, Wrist, Leg or Ankle)

- Cast/Cervical Collar -

- Cough Drops

- Ear Plugs

- EpiPen

- Eye Drops, Eye Patches

- Eye Glasses or Contacts

- Handled Magnifying Glass Non-electric, no case

- Ice Pack or Heating Pad

- Inhaler

- Insulin Pen, Glucose Tablets

- Medical Alert Bracelet

- Pillow/Lumbar Support

- Stool for Elevating Limb

- Surgical Face mask

- Tissue/ Kleenex

- Walking Boot Casts
