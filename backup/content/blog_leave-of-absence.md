### Intro

In this post I have consolidated all the information on taking a Leave of Absence (LOA) from Medical School and how to discuss it in your residency application. The below information comes from my own experience taking an LOA between my 2nd and 3rd year of medical school, and the results of multiple surveys of medical students who have similarly taken an LOA. The first survey (17 respondents) which found that 53% of respondents stated they were not given information from their school on important things to consider. Therefore, this article is written to meet this highly requested need.

If you would like to share your experience fill out this created with collaboration with Tufts Medical School.

[anonymous survey](https://tufts.qualtrics.com/jfe/form/SV_e9k44mFm9W3diaa)

Poster on LOA Presented at the 2025 American Psychiatric Association Meeting

### Types of LOA

Academic:

- The school usually requires or "highly recommends" a LOA

- Usually because one has failed too many courses, has to retake a year, or has to retake a board exam.

- This is seen not as favorably by residencies (confirmed by multiple Program Directors and Advisors)

- 53% of survey respondents

Personal:

- Can be for ANY reason (see below)

- Usually considered a "choice" to take a leave

- One does not need to explain WHY to their school

- 35% of survey respondents

- Do have to provide an explanation in the Residency Application Process (see below)

***When one takes a LOA from medical school, one must sign a piece of paper from administration check marking one of these two options

### Timeline to take an LOA:

Of respondents 41% took an LOA between 2nd and 3rd year and 29.4% took an LOA between their 3rd and 4th year of medical school.

### Considerations before taking an LOA:

Finances:

- How will you finance the months or year off?

- This is the #1 concern, fear, and challenge of students taking a LOA

- Funding options included personal savings, work, family support, and public financial aid assistance programs.

- Scroll down to about finances in medical school

[Part 5 of this blog post](https://medpsycmoss.com/blog/surviving-the-taboos-of-medical-school)

Student Loans:

- You are not allowed to take out Federal Student loans when you are on a LOA

- Your student loans will need to start being paid back after 6 months of LOA

- Can extend the holding period by contacting your loan provider and having the school send a form saying when you will return to school

- Student loans are taken away during LOA

- 38% of respondents (5/13) who took out student loans this was going to happen

didn’t know

47% of respondents were able to receive financial support

outside

50% received outside financial support from their parents, 16.7% from their partner/significant other, and 8.3% from family

53% of respondents had to work a job such as (tutoring, research lab, medical assistant, fitness instructor, babysitting, etc.)

The result of losing funding even resulted in declaring bankruptcy of a medical student because they could no longer afford to pay for their living expenses without their student loans

Financial resources:

Do you qualify for any financial aid assistance through city, state, or federal programs?

- Food stamps (SNAP) Application if you have NO family financial support or external income is under $0

- Food Banks

- State or city utilities Support

- WIC: If you or your partner is pregnant or have a young child

Health Insurance:

- How will you finance Health Insurance?

- Do you have a partner that you can go on their plan?

- Some schools allow you to remain on the same plan but you will need to pay out of pocket.

- For example mine cost $4,000 for 9 months off of school

- Are you eligible for Medicaid?

Disability Insurance:

- Most residency's recommend and provide disability insurance to residents because one never knows when a visible or invisible disability can arise which could impair you temporarily from working

- However, rarely do medical schools recommend, provide, or encourage students to get disability insurance

- The AAMC mandates medical schools have disability insurance available as a benefit to students but this is not advertised to students

- Most plans provide:

- $1,500/month to 1st & 2nd year medical students on LOA

- $2,000/month to 2nd & 3rd year medical students on LOA

- See an example of the benefit at in Chicago

[Loyola Medical School](https://www.luc.edu/bursar/medicalstudentdisabilityinsurance/)

Housing:

- Where will you live?

- Do you need to move back home or with a family member?

- Multiple students reported needing to give up their lease or housing to move in with family because they did not have the student loans to pay for rent

- Some students had to travel multiple states and pay out of pocket for travel and moving expenses

Check eligibility for Affordable Housing and Grants

Some students struggled so much with affording housing that they were at risk of being evicted for being late on making rent payments

### Residency Applications:

ERAS Online Application

- On the 2023 Residency Application this box was included: "Was your medical education/training extended or interrupted?" (510-characters)

- Since it is only 510 characters you have to be "short and sweet"

- Make sure to state facts and are honest

- Make sure to include 1-2 sentences of what one learned and how the experience was meaningful to their future career as a physician

- One can always include more in their personal statement, activities, and box for "Impactful Experiences" 750 character limit (with spaces)

Receiving Interviews

- I was told by a Residency Director that they can filter by students who have had NO MORE than 4 years of medical school, this means that an individual who has taken an LOA (an extra year of medical school) would be filtered out

- Out of the 44 programs I applied I have received ~20 interviews even though I have taken an LOA. However, I did receive rejections and haven't received replies from residency programs that might have filtered me out for my LOA

Interviewing

- Rarely have I been out-right asked about my LOA, but I have been asked about the topics sounding my LOA experience since it was a significant time point in my medical journey (health, financially, professionally, and academically).

- Specialties offering interviews to respondents who have taken LOA include Psychiatry, Family Medicine, and Neurology

Demonstrate to programs lessons learned and how the experience provided you skills that will make you a better physician such as resiliency, perseverance, and adaptability.
