### Finding The Right Pelvic Pain Provider

### 1. What type of Provider do you need?

Family Medicine or Internal Medcine- MD, DO, NP

Good start to ask about new pelvic pain or to get a refferal to a specialist

-

- Can help decide on birth control & family planning options

- Diagnose and treat sexually transmitted diseases

- Does pelvic and full body exams

OB/GYN- MD or DO

Can be fellowship trained in pelvic pain, sexual function, or urology (Uro-Gyn)

- Does pelvic exams with a scope, hand, and/or ultrasound

- Can help with picking contraceptives, family planning, and sexual health

- Can do Surgeries, Pain Injections and place nerve stimulators

- Checks for medical problems of individuals with vaginas and/or uterus includding organ prolapse

Urologist- MD or DO

- Does pelvic exams and can check for bladder function with a scope into the bladder

- Diagnosis and treats medical problems of the urinary system (urethra, bladder, ureters, and kidneys)

- Helps diagnose and treat reoccurent urinary tract infections, urinary incontinence, and bladder prolapse

- Can do Surgeries for the urinary system

- Diagnosis and treats medical problems of the intestines, does endoscopies & colonoscopies

Gastroenterologist MD or DO

- Helpful if experiencing problems (includding pain) with consistant constipation, diarhea, or bowel incontinence

Rectal Surgeon- MD or DO

- help with internal and external hemmroids

- does lower bowel surgeries

- Can be specially trained in pain injections and nerve stimulators

Anethesiologist - MD or DO

Plastic Surgeon- MD or DO

- Gender affirmation surgeries and reconstruction of sexual organs

Neurologist- MD or DO

- Helps diagnose and treat pain disorders rooted in nerves, spine, and brain

- Uses MRI, EEGs (tests electrical currents in brain), and EMGs (tests functioning of specific nerves and muscles)

Psychiatrist: MD or DO

- Helps provide medications and therapy to help neuropathic and body pains that can be exasperated by mental health disorders like anxiety, depression, PTSD...

- Help treat co-morbid substance use disorder

- Pharmaceutically treat mental health conditions

- Ph.D., Psy.D., Ed.D., Social Work

Psychologist

- trained for special types of therapy (ex: CBT, DBT, EMDR, Ketamine...)

- Can be trained in Sex Therapy

- Can be trained in Couples t

herapy

Physical Medicine & Rehabilitation Physician (PM&R)- MD/DO who works with a Pelvic Floor Therapist - PT

- Specially trained in the pelvic floor

- Internal & external strengthing and support of pelvic muscles

- Good for all pelvic pain conditions, bladder/bowel incontinence, and prolapse

- Can be trained to help with pesseries, vaginal/rectal dilators, and pelvic wands

- Can be trained to do injections, a form of cupping, and dry needling

### 3. Interview Providers

What type of training do you have?

Do you have specialized training in ______ treatment?

What therapies or philosphies do you follow?

How often would you anticipate seeing me? For how long?

Will I be always seeing you at appointments or will I be following up with a NP/PA/Nurse?

What types of goals do make for patients?

What does successful treatment look like for you?

What is a typical session like? How long are the sessions?

What kind of homework, exercises, or reading do you give patients?

Have you been in therapy yourself? Do you still? (for therapist)

How long have you been practicing?

How much do you charge? What are you sliding-scale options?

How many clients have you had with similar circumstances to my own?

How comfortable do you feel about working with ______(special population or circumstance)?

Keep a detailed diary of symptoms, the dates and times you experience them, and what makes them more or less severe. Be specific about the location and duration of pain. It’s also helpful to give your provider examples of how the pain is affecting you, for example, if it’s causing you to miss work. Bring a hard copy of your symptom log and ask your provider to add it to your medical record.

Work with a provider who has experience

Prepare for your appointment. and ask if your symptoms could be caused by endometriosis. You also may want to discuss what diagnostic tests will be recommended and what they involve, what treatment options are available, how endometriosis and any proposed treatment could affect your fertility, and if there any lifestyle changes that may help you manage symptoms.
