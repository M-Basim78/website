### Childhood Trauma Survivors

: In-Pt Psychiatric Floor

: Ran Group Therapy with a Workbook I helped develop, Individual Psychotherapy & Pharmaceutical Management

I spent the first part of my life in Colorado. In High School I enjoyed teaching martial arts, dancing and acting in school musicals, and learning STEM!

My husband, Jonah, and I met our senior year in high school while interviewing for a Leadership Program at the University of Colorado (CU) in Boulder. We were both accepted into the program and together decided to pursue our shared goal of medicine through completing our undergraduate degrees at CU.

Going to college with Jonah was a life saver after growing up in a dysfunctional household. My passion to understand trauma led me to pursue my Bachelor's of Arts in Psychology. In addition, the stressors of the pre-medical path led me to explore my passion for movement by getting a minor in dance.

Eventhough we were very blessed to have our parents save the money to pay for our college tuition, we needed to work in order to pay for our living expenses. Therefore, we lived and worked as Resident Advisors at the dormatories, and I also worked as a caregiver for the Elderly.

Once we graduated college, we took 2 gap years to get married, work full time, and apply to medical school. During this time I worked as a bilingual case manager for children with disabilities and retook the MCAT, which is the exam required to apply to medical school.

### Pre-med Timeline:

My husband and I were very blessed to be accepted into our favorite medical school, Rush Medical College, located in Chicago. We moved to Chicago with our dogs Midnight and Eclipse.

The first year of medical school was challenging, to say the least. During this time I pursued the help of a therapist. Through the help of weekly therapy sessions, I made it through the first year of medical school. Even with the onset of COVID-19 I maintained a positive attitude through pursuing meaninful leadership and volunteer experiences supporting the Chicago homeless community. This is when I co-founded the Chicago Homelessness and Health Response Group for Equity (CHHRGE).

The second year of medical school started with the Sex&Reproduction block which was the begining of uncovering my diagnosis of pelvic pain medical conditions. After multiple doctor appointments and a laproscopic sergury I was finally diagnosed with endometriosis and infertility.

M2 year I struggled greatly with my academics while also addressing my physicial and mental health conditions. After failing and successfully retaking two block exams, I decided to take a leave of absense (LOA) from medical school to foccus on my health.

During my LOA I took the first board exam, STEP1. Unfortuantly, I failed this exam by 1 point which was emotionally devasting. However, after a lot of healing, therapy, and studdying, I was able to retake it with accomidations for my medical condition. My LOA was very healing as I finally had the time to focus on healing physicially and mentally.

After months fighting with my insurance I was able to get my health insurance to cover about 80% of my ovarian egg stimulation and freezing. I am currently advocating for health insurance to cover all medical students to have their fertility medication covered if they need it. Since 1 in 4 female physicians struggle with infertility.

For work during my LOA,

I became a a movement instructor teaching Zumba, kickboxing, yoga, etc. to the Chicago underserved community. I taught hip hop to West side YMCA elementary school students, zumba for seniors at a neighboorhood center, and latin dance to the Latinx community.

I also worked as a medical assitant at a pain clinic which has brought me greater appreciation of the chronic pain world. Through this exerperience I witnessed medical gaslighting by my coworkers, which insighted another flame to educate the medical community on trauma-informed care.

In Spring 2022 I gave my first educational talks on trauma-informed medicine as a part of Ilinois Academy of Family Physicians (IAFP).
