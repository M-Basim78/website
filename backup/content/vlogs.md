The episode discusses the journey of Dr. Moss, a recently graduated medical student who was diagnosed with disabilities during their second year of medical school. It emphasizes the importance of requesting accommodations, provides examples of accommodations, explains laws regarding disability rights, and offers resources for navigating disabilities in medical education and residency programs.

In this episode, Stephanie Moss, a medical student shares their personal journey of failing the STEP exam, taking a leave of absence from medical school, and overcoming various challenges including financial difficulties and health issues. Through vulnerability and openness, they highlight the importance of sharing struggles, building community support, and redefining identity beyond societal stigmas. They conclude by encouraging listeners to reflect on their own experiences and the role of resilience in overcoming life's hardships.

This episode delves into the journey of Dr. Moss who shares valuable insights on overcoming failures in the USMLE Step 1 exam by emphasizing the importance of thorough preparation, meeting basic needs like physical, mental, and financial well-being, and utilizing effective study strategies. Thisunderscores the significance of learning from mistakes, utilizing resources like UWorld and First Aid, and the impact of questioning strategies, trust in knowledge, and external supports in academic success. It also highlights the crucial role of meeting basic needs such as physical and mental health, and financial stability in excelling in medical school and beyond.

In this video, Dr. Stephanie Moss interviews her husband, Dr. Jonah Moss, on the complexities of managing student loans during medical residency. They discuss various loan repayment options, including the Federal government's income-driven repayment plans, specifically the SAVE plan introduced during the Biden-Harris administration. Dr. Jonah Moss explains the difference between consolidation and refinancing, the implications of both, and shares his personal insights and experiences on handling student debt. They also touch on the benefits of filing taxes separately for lower monthly payments and emphasize the importance of not consolidating loans until after graduation. The episode concludes with practical advice tailored to medical residents navigating their financial futures.

In this video, Dr. Moss shares detailed guidance on how to create a compelling personal statement and navigate the medical residency application process. She emphasizes the importance of showing rather than telling through stories, incorporating patient encounters, demonstrating strengths and values, and preparing a solid CV. She also advises on networking at conferences, obtaining meaningful letters of recommendation, and utilizing various resources. Dr. Moss reflects on her journey, including overcoming personal challenges as a Latina with health conditions, to inspire and support future medical residents.

In this epsidode, Stephanie Moss, a medical student with invisible disabilities, discusses the challenges and lessons of medical school. She offers insights into mental health, academics, imposter syndrome, failing exams, accommodations for disabilities, taking a leave of absence, and finances. Moss emphasizes the importance of mental health, shares her own experiences, and provides resources for medical students. The script covers strategies for handling failure, understanding academic pressures, and managing financial realities in medical school.

This episode details the experiences of a Dr. Moss, a psychiatry resident, during their first two weeks of orientation. She discusses their program's emphasis on wellness, mental health, and work-life balance, especially as a doctor with disabilities. The transition to the VA connected to an active military base and work with a diverse patient population ranging from veterans to young recruits.

Dr. Moss expresses excitement about integrating various therapies, interacting with child and adolescent specialists, and participating in research. She shares the value of collaboration within interprofessional teams and the unique benefits provided through the military, such as comprehensive health resources. Additionally, the resident underscores the importance of humanizing patients, particularly those with substance use disorders, through personalized care and thoughtful interactions.

Dr. Moss, a PGY1 psychiatry resident, discusses the challenges and unspoken rules of the M3 clinical year for medical students. She emphasizes the importance of managing expectations with attending physicians and residents, focusing on specific patient assignments, and exercising good time management. Dr. Moss shares personal anecdotes from her experience, highlighting common pitfalls such as misunderstanding roles and failing to complete notes on time. The script also touches on the hidden curriculum within medical education and offers practical advice for new M3 students, including gathering history, asking questions rather than giving answers, and understanding the dynamics during group rounds.
