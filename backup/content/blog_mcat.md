### I Failed the MCAT, Retook It, and Got Into Multiple Medical Schools

An AMA Reflection for Premeds Who Feel Behind

I hosted an for premed students, and one theme came up again and again:

[Ask Me Anything (AMA) on Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/MCATprep/comments/1qol1qe/ama_i_failed_the_mcat_took_a_gap_year_and_still/?utm_source=share&utm_medium=web3x&utm_name=web3xcss&utm_term=1&utm_content=share_button)

“What do you do when you fail… and still want to be a doctor?”

So I wanted to turn those questions into a single place — especially for the premeds who feel like one exam, one score, or one bad day has already decided their future.

Here’s the truth: Retook it. Scored a 505. And still

I failed the MCAT (around 475).

got into multiple medical schools.

Now I am a Medical Doctor and Chief Psychiatry Resident for Medical Student Education.

This is what that actually looked like.

Q: What Do I Mean When I Say I “Failed” the MCAT?

A: When people hear “failed the MCAT,” they often assume I mean I got a score I was unhappy with.

No — I mean I truly failed.

This was a decade ago, but I’m fairly sure my first MCAT score was around a 475, which at the time was essentially the lowest possible score. I had a panic attack during the exam and didn’t finish any of the sections on time.

It wasn’t a knowledge issue. It wasn’t a motivation issue. It was an untreated testing disability + anxiety + zero accommodations issue.

At the time, though, all I saw was .

failure

Q: How Did you Re-Study for the MCAT the Second Time?

A: This part is simple, but uncomfortable: I stopped doing what felt "productive" like watching Videos & Lectures and started doing what was actually effective ( )

QUESTIONS

Re-studying meant:

- Analyzing why types of questions and topics I get wrong ( does great at tracking this)

[UWorld](https://www.awin1.com/cread.php?awinmid=120129&awinaffid=2844780)

- Identifying testing patterns, not just content gaps (for me was physics & CARS)

- Adjusting how I learned, not just how long (time) I studied

Progress didn’t come from grinding harder — it came from learning differently

Q: Did medical schools question your failure or retake? Did interviewers grill you about it?

A: Honestly? I don’t remember being asked much at all.

Here's what many applicants don’t realize this:

Interviewers usually don’t see your scores

They often only have your personal statement and activities

Admissions committees review metrics (GPA/MCAT Scores) ; interviewers assess YOU (as a person)

If a ever does come up, it’s less about the failure and more about how you reflect on it. Growth matters far more than perfection.

Q: How Did You Mentally Recover From Failing?

Exam failure doesn’t just hurt academically — it attacks your .

identity

You start thinking:

“Maybe I’m not smart enough”

“Maybe this is a sign”

“Maybe everyone else was right”

I didn’t magically bounce back. It took time, therapy, and healing.

What helped was slowly separating:

“I failed an exam”

from

“I am a failure.”

That reframing took YEARS — and later resurfaced again when I failed USMLE Step 1 in medical school.

Failure didn’t disappear after getting accepted. But neither did resilience.

Q: How did you Retake the MCAT While Working

One of my favorite questions came from a non-traditional student with a family who was working full-time: “How did you juggle MCAT studying while working and just living life?”

A: The honest answer:

structure and communication.

I sat down and mapped out:

- Work hours

- Class time

- Study blocks

- Rest and relationship time

My partner (also a pre-med who was working full time), and I intentionally aligned our schedules so that:

- We worked at the same time

- Studied at the same time

- Took time off together

It wasn’t perfect — but it was intentional, and that made it sustainable.

If you want to learn more about how we continued this in medical school check out this

[blog post from 1st year as a MS1.](https://medpsycmoss.com/blog/ms1)

Q: What do Testing Accommodations look like for the MCAT vs. STEP Exams

I was asked about accommodations a lot — especially by students planning ahead.

From helping dozens of medical students and premeds, I can say:

MCAT, USMLE, and COMLEX accommodations are very similar

Requirements are strict

Documentation matters

Language matters

I didn’t get accommodations early — and that cost me time, confidence, and unnecessary suffering. Helping others avoid that has become one of the most meaningful parts of my work.

Q: Did You Struggle Later in Medical School and Matching?

Yes.... I failed USMLE Step 1 twice, took a leave of absence, and only passed after receiving proper accommodations.

And yet — I still

matched.

Setbacks didn’t disqualify me. They reshaped how I advocate, mentor, and show up for others.

Final Thoughts for Premeds Reading This

If you take nothing else from this post, let it be this:

- A failed MCAT does define your intelligence

not

- A low score does erase your potential

- A non-traditional path is a weakness

- Accommodations are cheating — they’re access

You Deserve to Demonstrate Your Best

- You are allowed to need support.

- You are allowed to take time.

- And you are allowed to keep going.

Your story isn’t over — even if this chapter hurts.
