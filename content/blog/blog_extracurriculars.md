How to Navigate Extracurricular Activities as a Pre-Med or Medical Student

Extracurricular activities are an expected part of applying to medical school and residency—but figuring out which ones actually matter (and how many is “enough”) can feel overwhelming. Many students fall into the trap of overloading their schedules with activities that look good on paper, only to end up burned out and stretched too thin.

In this post, I’ll break down the different types of extracurriculars, how to choose opportunities that genuinely strengthen your application, and how to be intentional so your involvement tells a clear story—without sacrificing your academics or well-being. Whether you’re a pre-med just getting started or a medical student refining your CV, this guide will help you focus on what truly adds value.

Choosing the Right Activities: Quality Over Quantity

When it comes to extracurriculars, there’s no single “perfect” checklist—but there is a common theme among strong applications: intentional involvement. Activities should support your growth, align with your values, and tell a clear story about who you are and why medicine makes sense for you.

Guiding Principles for Choosing Activities

Do what you love. If you’re not passionate about it, it will show—and burnout will follow.

Choose activities that are in service of others, especially communities in need.

Seek opportunities to work with diverse patient populations.

Work in team-based and interprofessional environments.

Demonstrate different forms of leadership (formal and informal).

Only continue activities that bring you purpose, joy, and meaning.

Practice balancing academics with extracurriculars—grades still matter.

Learn when to say “no” and when it’s okay to step back.

Show long-term commitment by staying involved over multiple years whenever possible.

Tying Your Extracurriculars Into Your Application

Choose one or two volunteer roles you genuinely care about and commit long-term. Depth, reflection, and service to others matter far more than checking every box. Then over time take on a leadership role. Lastly, turn the project into research & present at a conference!

Waiving Extracurriculars into Your Story

Your extracurriculars are not meant to stand alone—they exist to support your story. Medical schools are not looking for a list of impressive titles; they are looking for evidence that your experiences have shaped how you think, how you serve others, and why medicine is the right path for you.

When writing your personal statement, focus less on what you did and more on what you learned. The strongest narratives show growth: how working with vulnerable populations deepened your empathy, how team-based roles taught you communication and humility, or how leadership experiences helped you navigate responsibility and accountability. Your activities should naturally reinforce the themes already present in your story.

Which experiences best explain why I want to be a physician?

How did these activities challenge or change me?

What moments confirmed that medicine aligns with my values?

Your personal statement should feel like the throughline connecting your extracurriculars—demonstrating consistency, reflection, and purpose. When your activities, reflections, and motivations align, admissions committees can clearly see not only that you are prepared for medicine, but why you belong in it.

Remember: a strong application isn’t built on doing more—it’s built on doing what matters, staying committed, and being able to thoughtfully articulate how those experiences shaped you.
